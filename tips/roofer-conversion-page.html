Roofer conversion page for storm leads and replacements

Roofing leads arrive after a storm, a drip in the ceiling, or a neighbor’s new roof. A conversion page meets that moment with clear services, local coverage, and one way to reach you — not a 12-page site rebuild.

Separate storm / leak / replacement

Put emergency leak and storm inspection near the top. Then list full replacements and maintenance. Homeowners under a leak will not scroll through portfolio galleries first.

Proof that looks like your work

Job photos of shingles, flat roofs, or flashing you actually installed beat generic stock. Add insurance or license notes without inventing reviews.

Towns and materials, plainly

List the towns you cover and the roof types you take (asphalt, flat, metal if true). Vague “tri-state coverage” pages underperform local roofing searches.

CTA that survives mobile

Storm searchers are often on a phone in the driveway. Tap-to-call first; email second. Keep the form to name, phone, town, and a short description of the issue.

Launch friction check

  • Tap-to-call in the hero for storm and leak traffic.
  • Towns served are explicit near the top.
  • Roof types listed without jargon walls.
  • No fake testimonials — use real job photos.
  • Title and H1 match roof repair or replacement intent.
