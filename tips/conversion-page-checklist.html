Conversion page checklist for local businesses and contractors

A conversion page for a local business is not a brochure. It is one focused page that answers “can you help me in my town?” and makes calling or emailing the obvious next step. Use this landing page checklist before you spend on ads or redesign.

1. Lead with the job and the town

Your headline should name the service and where you work. “Emergency plumber in Wayne, NJ” beats “Quality solutions since 1998.” Visitors from search or a referral decide in seconds whether the page is for them.

2. One primary call to action

Pick call or email as the main action and repeat it in the hero, mid-page, and footer. Extra buttons for social feeds, blogs, and “learn more” dilute bookings. A conversion page for local business wins when the CTA is boring and obvious.

3. Proof that fits trades work

Use what you already have: recent job photos, license or insurance notes, years in the area, and a short list of towns you serve. Skip fake star quotes. Real specifics outperform generic praise.

4. Service-area clarity

List the towns or counties you cover near the top. Local searchers bounce when they cannot tell if you drive to their street. Pair that with a simple “what we fix / what we build” list so the page matches the query.

5. Friction check before launch

  • Phone is tap-to-call on mobile (tel: link).
  • Email opens a pre-filled subject so the lead is ready to send.
  • Page loads without a popup wall on first visit.
  • No DOB, SSN, or unnecessary forms — collect name, contact, and job details only.
  • Footer has business name, town, and contact (street address optional for privacy).
  • Title and meta description match the page promise for search snippets.

6. Ship a thin page first

Contractors often wait on a full website. A single landing page checklist for contractors is enough to start: hero, three proof points, services, service area, FAQ, and CTA. You can expand later. Speed to a live URL usually beats a perfect homepage that never launches.
